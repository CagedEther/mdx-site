# How Solar Power Works

Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity through the **photovoltaic effect** — when photons from sunlight knock electrons loose in a silicon cell, creating a flow of electricity.

## The basic system

A typical home solar installation has four main parts:

- **Solar panels** — capture sunlight and produce DC electricity
- **Inverter** — converts DC to AC electricity your home can use
- **Net meter** — tracks how much electricity you send back to the grid
- **Electrical panel** — distributes power throughout your home

## How your bill changes

Once your system is installed, your utility bill reflects only the electricity you pull from the grid *minus* the credits you've earned by sending solar power back. On sunny months, many homeowners receive a bill of $0 — or even a credit.

## What affects your production

| Factor | Impact |
|---|---|
| Panel orientation | South-facing panels produce up to 20% more than east/west |
| Roof pitch | 30–40° tilt is optimal in most of the US |
| Shading | Even partial shade on one panel can reduce output significantly |
| Temperature | Panels are most efficient in cold, sunny conditions |

## The 25-year picture

Solar panels degrade slowly — typically 0.5% per year. A system that produces 10,000 kWh in year one will produce around 8,800 kWh in year 25. Our calculator models this degradation curve accurately so your long-term projections are realistic.
